# Chapter 1 — The Human Question

:::note What you'll be able to do
After this chapter you can explain, in your own words, **why understanding AI
starts with understanding human intelligence**, and describe the basic cycle that
human intelligence runs through.
:::

## The plain explanation

### Why begin with humans?

To understand artificial intelligence, you first need a clear point of
comparison: human beings. Before this book explains what AI *is*, it starts with
what makes a person a person — because human beings and human intelligence are
the baseline against which AI is measured, described, and marketed.

When people say an AI system is "smart", "creative", or "reasoning", every one of
those words is borrowed from humans. If you do not have a clear picture of what
those words mean for a person, you cannot tell whether they are being used
accurately for a machine.

Being human involves a set of capacities that are easy to take for granted:

- **Lived experience** — a history of moments that shapes how you see the world.
- **Needs and motivations** — reasons for acting that come from within, not from
  a prompt.
- **Emotion and relationships** — feelings that connect you to other people and
  shape decisions.
- **Values and ethical judgement** — a sense of right and wrong you can reason
  about and defend.
- **Imagination and purpose** — the ability to picture what does not exist yet
  and work toward it.
- **Responsibility for decisions and outcomes** — being answerable for what you
  choose to do.
- **The ability to create tools and institutions** — including AI itself.

At the centre of most of these is **human intelligence**.

### What is human intelligence?

:::info Definition
Human intelligence is the natural human capacity to perceive, learn, reason,
feel, imagine, make decisions, solve problems, and adapt through experience.
:::

Human intelligence is not one isolated ability. It emerges from many connected
parts of being human: biology, perception, memory, emotions, needs, experiences,
relationships, values, and the ability to reflect on our own actions.

In simple terms, it lets us move through a continuous cycle:

**Perceive → Interpret → Remember → Reason → Decide → Act → Learn**

We take in information from the world, interpret what it means, connect it with
what we already know, consider possible actions, make a choice, act, and learn
from what happens next. Then the cycle runs again, slightly changed by what we
just learned.

### Intelligence is also collective

Human intelligence does not operate only inside one head. Through communication
and cooperation, people share knowledge, coordinate action, and build things no
individual could create alone:

**Relationships → Communities → Cultures → Organisations → Institutions →
Knowledge → Tools**

And among the tools humans have created is artificial intelligence. AI does not
appear at the beginning of this story. It appears much later.

> Humans came first. Natural intelligence came first. Artificial intelligence is
> something humans created.

That is why this book begins with natural human intelligence: to understand AI
clearly, we first need to understand the intelligence it is designed to
reproduce, extend, or support — and, just as importantly, what stays distinctly
human.

## A worked example

Trace the cycle through one ordinary decision: **you are driving and the traffic
light ahead turns amber.**

| Step | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Perceive | Your eyes register the light changing colour. |
| Interpret | "Amber — the light is about to turn red." |
| Remember | You recall how far back you are, your speed, that there is a car close behind you, and a past near-miss from braking too hard. |
| Reason | Stopping safely is possible but abrupt; continuing is legal and safe if you are already close. You weigh both. |
| Decide | You choose to continue through. |
| Act | You maintain speed and pass under the light. |
| Learn | Nothing went wrong, which slightly reinforces that judgement for next time. Had a pedestrian stepped out, the lesson — and the next decision — would be different. |

Notice how much of this leans on things that are not in the amber light itself:
your memory, your past experiences, your sense of risk, your responsibility for
the outcome. The perception is the easy part. The rest is where human
intelligence does its work.

## Key terms

- **Human intelligence** — the natural human capacity to perceive, learn, reason,
  feel, imagine, decide, solve problems, and adapt through experience.
- **The intelligence cycle** — Perceive → Interpret → Remember → Reason → Decide
  → Act → Learn; a continuous loop, not a one-off.
- **Collective intelligence** — knowledge and capability that emerge when people
  communicate and cooperate, exceeding what any individual could achieve.
- **Baseline for comparison** — the reference point (here, human intelligence)
  against which a new thing (here, AI) is described and judged.
